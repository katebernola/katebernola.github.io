How I coach

Expect great work +

I'm a perfectionist. I don't let work ship when something's wrong. If a designer layers something onto the UI that could be built into it, it goes back.

Name the problem +

When I see a flawed design, I don't dwell on the mistakes. I identify the design heuristic or principle that's broken.

Let them solve it +

Then I get out of the way. I only hand over the answer when a deadline is imminent, or when they've tried several times and the ask is genuinely out of their league. That's how you get quality without micromanaging.

Lead by example +

When I take on a new team, I take a hard problem myself, visibly. That gives them permission to challenge the ask, rethink the problem, and take bigger swings.

Pull in specialists +

If I say "Get Alex's help here," designers try to do it themselves, because they think needing help counts against their work. If I say "Great structure; Alex should do the visual design," that takes the decision off them. Nobody has to be great at everything. Unless it's a skill they're actively looking to develop, I bring in an expert who gets to great in half the time.
